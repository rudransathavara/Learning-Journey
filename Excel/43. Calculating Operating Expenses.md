# Calculating Operating Expenses in Excel

## Overview

Operating expenses, often abbreviated to OPEX, are the costs associated with running a business that are not included in the cost of goods sold.

Examples include:

- Personnel expenses
- Human resources costs
- Rent and office expenses
- Marketing expenses
- Depreciation and amortisation

In a financial model, operating expenses can be forecast using assumptions expressed as a percentage of revenue.

## 1. Calculate Personnel Expenses

Suppose:

- Net revenue is in cell `B9`.
- The personnel expense assumption is in cell `B43`.
- Personnel expenses are calculated in cell `B21`.

Enter:

`=B9*B$43`

This multiplies net revenue by the assumed personnel expense percentage.

For example, if net revenue is £1,000,000 and personnel expenses are forecast at 25% of net revenue:

£1,000,000 × 25% = £250,000

## 2. Understand Mixed Cell References

The formula uses `B$43`.

The dollar sign fixes row 43 while allowing the column to change.

When copied across:

- `B$43` becomes `C$43`.
- `C$43` becomes `D$43`.

This allows the formula to reference the appropriate assumption row in each forecast year.

Press **F4** while editing a cell reference to cycle through the available reference types.

## 3. Copy the Formula

Once the first formula is correct:

1. Select the cell containing the formula.
2. Use the fill handle to copy it across the forecast years.
3. Copy it down for other expense categories where the same calculation method applies.
4. Check that each expense references the correct assumption.

## 4. Depreciation and Amortisation

Depreciation allocates the cost of a tangible asset over its useful life.

Amortisation applies a similar approach to eligible intangible assets, such as certain software assets.

These expenses are linked to the capital expenditure and depreciation schedule.

If that schedule has not yet been completed, flag the relevant cells for later calculation rather than entering unsupported values.

## 5. Calculate Total Operating Expenses

If the operating expense items occupy cells `B21:B24`, enter the following in `B25`:

`=SUM(B21:B24)`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

The total will update automatically when the individual expenses change.

Remember that total operating expenses will be incomplete until all relevant expenses, including depreciation and amortisation, have been included.

## 6. Formatting

- Use consistent number formats for all expense items.
- Use bold formatting for total operating expenses.
- Apply borders to distinguish subtotals.
- Use temporary highlighting for incomplete calculations.
- Remove temporary highlighting when the relevant formulas are completed.

## Key Takeaways

- Operating expenses represent the costs of running a business.
- Expenses can be forecast as a percentage of net revenue.
- Mixed references help formulas work correctly when copied across.
- Total operating expenses are calculated using the `SUM` function.
- Depreciation and amortisation should be linked from the relevant schedule.
