# Financial Modelling Assumptions in Excel

## Overview

Financial modelling assumptions are the inputs used to calculate projected financial results.

In an income statement model, assumptions may include customer growth, average order value, refunds, discounts, production costs, operating expenses and tax rates.

Clearly organising assumptions makes a financial model easier to understand, update and audit.

## Why Are Assumptions Important?

Assumptions help a business:

- Estimate future revenue.
- Forecast operating costs.
- Model potential profit and loss.
- Compare different business scenarios.
- Understand how changes in key inputs affect financial performance.
- Build a foundation for a wider three-statement financial model.

The quality of a forecast depends partly on the quality and reasonableness of its assumptions.

## Main Categories of Assumptions

The cookie company example uses the following categories.

### 1. Revenue Assumptions

These determine how much revenue the company expects to generate.

| Assumption | Purpose |
|---|---|
| New Customers | Estimates the number of new customers in each period. |
| Average Order Value | Estimates the average amount spent per order. |
| Refunds as a Percentage of Revenue | Estimates the proportion of revenue refunded. |
| Discounts as a Percentage of Revenue | Estimates the proportion of revenue deducted through discounts. |

These assumptions help calculate gross and net revenue.

### 2. Cost of Goods Sold Assumptions

COGS assumptions estimate the direct costs associated with producing and delivering goods.

Examples include:

- Materials costs
- Fulfilment costs
- Merchant services fees

These may be modelled as percentages of revenue or using another suitable cost driver.

The model should clearly identify which measure each percentage is based on.

### 3. Operating Expense Assumptions

Operating expense assumptions estimate the costs required to run the business.

Examples include:

- Human resources
- Marketing
- Miscellaneous expenses
- Depreciation
- Interest expense

Depreciation and interest may require separate calculations in a more complete financial model.

For example, depreciation may depend on the business's fixed assets and capital expenditure, while interest expense may depend on outstanding debt and interest rates.

### 4. Tax Rate

The tax rate is used to estimate tax expense.

A simplified model may calculate tax using a single assumed percentage of the relevant profit measure.

A detailed model may need to account for tax losses, allowances, jurisdiction-specific rules and other adjustments.

## Organising Assumptions in Excel

A clear structure makes it easier to maintain the model.

### Step 1: Group Related Assumptions

Separate the assumptions into logical categories, such as:

- Revenue
- COGS
- Operating Expenses
- Tax

### Step 2: Include Periods

Place the relevant periods in separate columns.

For example:

| Assumption | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| New Customers | Input | Input | Input | Input |
| Average Order Value | Input | Input | Input | Input |
| Refund Rate | Input | Input | Input | Input |
| Discount Rate | Input | Input | Input | Input |

The actual values should come from your business assumptions or practice workbook.

### Step 3: Distinguish Inputs from Formulas

Use consistent formatting to distinguish hard-coded assumptions from calculated values.

A common financial modelling convention is:

- Blue font for hard-coded inputs.
- Black font for formulas.
- Green font for references to other worksheets.

These conventions are not universal, so use the style required by your organisation or project.

### Step 4: Document Assumptions

Where appropriate, record:

- The source of each assumption.
- The date the assumption was prepared.
- The reasoning behind the value.
- Any limitations or uncertainties.

This helps users understand the basis of the forecast.

## How Assumptions Feed into the Income Statement

The assumptions drive the formulas used in the income statement.

### Gross Revenue

`Gross Revenue = New Customers × Average Order Value`

### Refunds

`Refunds = -Gross Revenue × Refund Rate`

### Discounts

`Discounts = -Gross Revenue × Discount Rate`

### Net Revenue

`Net Revenue = Gross Revenue + Refunds + Discounts`

### Individual COGS Categories

For a cost category modelled as a percentage of net revenue:

`Cost = Net Revenue × Cost Percentage`

### Total COGS

`Total COGS = SUM(Individual COGS Categories)`

### Gross Profit

`Gross Profit = Net Revenue − Total COGS`

### Gross Margin

`Gross Margin = Gross Profit ÷ Net Revenue`

These relationships allow the model to recalculate automatically when assumptions change.

## Using Fixed References

When formulas are copied across periods, some assumptions may need to remain fixed.

Excel supports several reference types:

| Reference | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `B6` | Both row and column can change when copied. |
| `$B$6` | Both column and row remain fixed. |
| `B$6` | Row remains fixed; column can change. |
| `$B6` | Column remains fixed; row can change. |

Use the appropriate reference type for the formula you are building.

A fixed reference should only be used when the model requires that particular row, column or cell to remain constant.

## Scenario Analysis

Once the assumptions are organised, you can test how changes affect the forecast.

For example, you could compare:

- Higher and lower customer growth.
- Changes in average order value.
- Increased refund rates.
- Higher materials costs.
- Changes in marketing expenditure.

This helps identify which assumptions have the greatest impact on revenue and profitability.

## Important Tips

- **Use realistic assumptions:** Base inputs on reasonable evidence or clearly stated estimates.
- **Keep assumptions consistent:** Ensure each period uses the intended values.
- **Use clear labels:** Every assumption should have a meaningful name and unit.
- **Separate inputs from calculations:** This makes the model easier to update.
- **Document sources:** Record where important assumptions came from.
- **Test different scenarios:** Understand how sensitive the forecast is to changes.
- **Check the formulas:** Confirm that each assumption feeds into the correct calculation.

## Key Takeaways

- Assumptions are the inputs that drive a financial model.
- Revenue, COGS, operating expenses and tax are key categories.
- Clear formatting helps distinguish inputs from formulas.
- Absolute and mixed references help control formula behaviour.
- Scenario analysis shows how changes in assumptions affect financial results.
- Documented assumptions improve transparency and make a model easier to review.

Use structured assumptions when preparing financial forecasts, testing business scenarios or building an income statement that will eventually feed into a complete three-statement financial model.
