# Stock Charts in Excel

## Overview

A **stock chart** is used to visualise stock market data over time.

Depending on the chart type, it can display values such as:

- Opening price
- Closing price
- Highest price
- Lowest price
- Trading volume

Stock charts help users examine price movements, compare trading ranges and visualise changes in market prices.

## Why Use Stock Charts?

- Visualise stock price movements over time.
- Compare high and low prices.
- Identify broad trends and periods of volatility.
- Present financial market data in a compact visual format.

## Preparing the Data

Before creating a stock chart:

1. Organise the data into columns.
2. Give each column a clear heading.
3. Ensure the values are numeric where appropriate.
4. Arrange the data in the order required by the chosen stock chart type.
5. Include dates or other time periods when needed to identify the observations.

**Important:** Excel stock charts require a specific data structure. The selected chart type must match the columns available in your dataset.

## How to Create a High-Low-Close Stock Chart

### Step 1: Select the Data

Open the `Stock Chart` practice workbook.

Select the data range:

`A3:D21`

In the example, columns B, C and D contain the High, Low and Close values.

Make sure the data is arranged in the order expected by the chart type.

### Step 2: Insert the Stock Chart

1. Go to the **Insert** tab.
2. Find the **Charts** group.
3. Open the chart menu containing **Waterfall, Funnel, Stock, Surface and Radar** charts.
4. Locate the **Stock** chart options.
5. Hover over the available types to preview them.
6. Select **High-Low-Close**.

Excel creates the stock chart using the selected data.

### Step 3: Resize the Chart

To make the chart easier to read:

1. Click the chart.
2. Drag one of its corner handles outwards.
3. Adjust its size to fit the worksheet or report.

Avoid making the chart so small that the price movements become difficult to interpret.

## Adding a Trendline

A trendline can help show the general direction of a data series.

### Step 1: Select the Chart

Click the stock chart to display its chart controls.

### Step 2: Open Chart Elements

1. Look for the green **plus (+)** button beside the chart.
2. Click it to open **Chart Elements**.
3. Enable **Trendline**, if available.

### Step 3: Choose the Data Series

Excel may allow you to choose which series receives the trendline.

For example, you might select the High series.

The trendline is added to that series.

**Note:** A trendline summarises a pattern. It does not reliably predict future stock prices or establish why prices changed.

## Formatting the Trendline

To customise its appearance:

1. Click the trendline.
2. Open the **Format** tab or the trendline formatting options.
3. Change the line colour if desired.
4. Adjust the line width or style.

Choose formatting that makes the trendline easy to distinguish from the original data.

## Changing the Chart Style

Excel provides several built-in styles to improve chart readability.

1. Select the chart.
2. Open the **Chart Design** tab.
3. Browse the available chart styles.
4. Select a style that makes the data clear.
5. Use the chart colour options to adjust the colour scheme if necessary.

Formatting should improve readability rather than distract from the financial information.

## Important Tips

- **Use the correct chart type:** A High-Low-Close chart requires the corresponding data fields.
- **Check the column order:** Stock charts are sensitive to how the source data is arranged.
- **Verify the selected range:** Make sure you have included the intended headings and observations.
- **Label the data clearly:** Dates and meaningful column headings help users understand the chart.
- **Interpret trends carefully:** Historical price movements do not guarantee future performance.

## Key Takeaways

- Stock charts visualise price movements over time.
- The High-Low-Close chart displays the high, low and closing prices.
- Trendlines can help highlight broad patterns in a selected series.
- Chart styles and formatting can improve readability.
- The source data must follow the structure required by the selected stock chart type.
