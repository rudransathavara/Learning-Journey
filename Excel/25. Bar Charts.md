# Bar Charts in Excel

## 1. What Is a Bar Chart?

Bar charts and column charts are used to present data visually, making it easier to understand comparisons and patterns.

They are useful for:
- Comparing values across different categories.
- Showing changes over a period of time.
- Presenting spreadsheet data in a visual format.
- Helping people understand information without having to examine the entire spreadsheet.

### Bar Charts vs. Column Charts

Excel distinguishes between bar charts and column charts based on the direction of the bars.

| Chart Type | Appearance | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| Column chart | Vertical bars | Comparing categories or showing changes over time. |
| Bar chart | Horizontal bars | Comparing values across different categories. |

In a typical column chart:
- The **horizontal axis (X-axis)** displays categories.
- The **vertical axis (Y-axis)** displays numerical values.

In a typical bar chart, these orientations are reversed.

## 2. Preparing Your Data

Before creating a chart, decide which information you want to display.

**The key is to select only the data needed for the chart.**

In the practice file, the relevant data is located in cells `A3:G7`.

### What Should You Exclude?

Avoid selecting unnecessary information, such as:

- **Extra titles:** A large merged heading, such as the one in cell `A1`, may not be needed because it is separate from the actual data table.
- **Totals:** Including a totals row, such as row 8 in the practice file, could distort the visual comparison between individual categories and make the chart harder to interpret.

For example, instead of selecting the entire worksheet, select only the relevant data range:

```excel
A3:G7
```

This tells Excel exactly which data to use when creating the chart.

**Tip:** Include the column and row headings that help identify your categories and data series, but exclude unrelated information.

## 3. How to Create a Column or Bar Chart

Follow these steps to create a chart in Excel.

### Step 1: Select Your Data

1. Open your Excel worksheet.
2. Highlight the data you want to visualize.
3. Include the relevant headings.
4. Exclude unnecessary titles and totals.

In the practice file, select:

```excel
A3:G7
```

### Step 2: Open the Insert Tab

1. Click the **Insert** tab on the Excel ribbon.
2. Locate the **Charts** group.
3. Find the **Insert Column or Bar Chart** button.

### Step 3: Choose a Chart Type

Click the **Insert Column or Bar Chart** button to view the available chart types.

Excel offers several options, including:

- **2-D Column charts**
- **3-D Column charts**
- **2-D Bar charts**
- **3-D Bar charts**

Column charts appear in the column chart section, while horizontal bar charts appear in the bar chart section.

### Step 4: Preview the Chart

Hover over a chart subtype to see a live preview of how your selected data will look.

This allows you to compare the available chart styles before choosing one.

### Step 5: Insert the Chart

Click the chart subtype you want.

Excel creates the chart and places it directly on top of your worksheet cells.

This is called an **embedded chart**.

## 4. What Is an Embedded Chart?

An embedded chart is a chart placed directly inside a worksheet rather than on a separate chart sheet.

It appears on top of the existing cells, so you can move and resize it without changing the underlying data.

Embedded charts are useful because:
- They keep the chart close to its source data.
- They make worksheets more visual.
- They allow you to compare the chart with the original numbers.
- They can be repositioned to improve the worksheet layout.

## 5. How to Resize a Chart

After inserting a chart, you may need to adjust its size.

### Steps to Resize a Chart

1. Click the chart to select it.
2. Look for the sizing handles around its edges and corners.
3. Move your pointer over a corner handle.
4. Click and drag inward to make the chart smaller, or outward to make it larger.
5. Release the mouse button when the chart reaches the desired size.

**Tip:** Dragging a corner handle is a convenient way to resize the chart while maintaining its general proportions.

## 6. How to Move a Chart

If the chart covers your data or takes up too much space, you can move it to another part of the worksheet.

### Steps to Move a Chart

1. Click the chart to select it.
2. Move your pointer over the chart's border, away from the sizing handles.
3. When the pointer indicates that the chart can be moved, click and drag it.
4. Position the chart where you want it.
5. Release the mouse button.

You can move the chart to an empty area of the worksheet so that it does not obscure important information.

### Deselecting and Selecting a Chart

- To **deselect** the chart, click a cell outside it.
- To **select** the chart again, click anywhere on its border or inside the chart area.

When selected, the chart displays its sizing handles, allowing you to move or resize it.

## 7. Why Are Charts Useful?

Charts make data easier to understand by presenting information visually.

Instead of asking someone to examine every number in a spreadsheet, you can use a chart to highlight comparisons and patterns.

For example, a column chart could help someone quickly compare sales across several regions.

Charts are particularly useful for:
- Reports and presentations.
- Comparing categories.
- Identifying patterns in data.
- Summarizing large amounts of information.
- Communicating results to people who do not need every underlying detail.

**Remember:** A chart should make your data easier to understand, not more complicated.

## 8. Important Things to Remember

- **Select the correct data range.** Include the information you want to visualize and exclude unnecessary titles or totals.
- **Choose an appropriate chart type.** Column charts use vertical bars, while bar charts use horizontal bars.
- **Use the Insert tab.** The Charts group contains the options for inserting column and bar charts.
- **Preview chart subtypes.** Hover over an option to see how your data will appear.
- **An embedded chart sits on top of worksheet cells.** You can move and resize it independently of the data.
- **Keep charts readable.** Position them so they do not cover important information.
- **Focus on clarity.** The purpose of a chart is to communicate the important information quickly.

## 9. Summary

To create a bar or column chart in Excel:

1. Select the data you want to display.
2. Go to **Insert**.
3. Open the **Insert Column or Bar Chart** menu in the Charts group.
4. Preview the available chart subtypes.
5. Select the chart you want.
6. Move or resize the embedded chart as needed.

**In summary:** Bar and column charts help you compare values and present data visually. Selecting the right data and chart type makes your information easier to understand.
