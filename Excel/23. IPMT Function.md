# IPMT Function in Excel

## 1. What Is the IPMT Function?

The **IPMT (Interest Payment)** function calculates the interest portion of a loan payment for a specific payment period.

When you repay a loan, each payment generally consists of two parts:

- **Principal:** The amount that reduces the original loan balance.
- **Interest:** The cost of borrowing the money.

The IPMT function helps you determine how much of a payment goes toward interest during a particular period.

It is commonly used in loan amortization schedules to track interest payments over the life of a loan.

## 2. IPMT Function Syntax

```excel
=IPMT(rate, per, nper, pv, [fv], [type])
```

### Arguments

| Argument | Description | Required? |
|---|---|---|
| `rate` | The interest rate for each payment period. | Yes |
| `per` | The specific payment period for which you want to calculate the interest. | Yes |
| `nper` | The total number of payment periods over the loan's duration. | Yes |
| `pv` | The present value, or the original amount borrowed. | Yes |
| `fv` | The desired balance after the final payment. Usually `0` for a fully repaid loan. | No |
| `type` | Specifies when payments are due: `0` or omitted means at the end of each period; `1` means at the beginning. | No |

The first four arguments are required. The `fv` and `type` arguments are optional.

## 3. Understanding the Loan Information

In the practice file, the worksheet contains information such as the interest rate, payment period, loan term, and original loan amount.

For the first example, the relevant cells are:

| Cell | Description |
|---|---|
| `B2` | Annual interest rate |
| `B3` | Payment period to calculate |
| `B4` | Loan term in years |
| `B5` | Original loan amount |

Because the first example calculates monthly interest, the annual interest rate and loan term must be converted into monthly values.

### Convert the Annual Interest Rate

If the interest rate is annual but payments are monthly, divide the annual rate by 12:

```excel
=B2/12
```

For example, if the annual interest rate is 8%:

`8% / 12 = approximately 0.6667% per month`

### Calculate the Total Number of Payments

Multiply the loan term in years by 12:

```excel
=B4*12
```

For example, a three-year loan with monthly payments has:

`3 × 12 = 36 payments`

The interest rate and total number of payments must correspond to the same payment frequency.

## 4. Example 1: Calculating Interest for the First Month

Suppose you want to calculate the interest portion of the first monthly payment.

### Step 1: Select a Cell

Click the cell where you want the result to appear.

### Step 2: Enter the Formula

Using the worksheet cells from the lesson, enter:

```excel
=IPMT(B2/12, B3, B4*12, B5)
```

This formula calculates the interest portion of the payment for the period specified in cell `B3`.

The arguments are:

- `B2/12` — the monthly interest rate.
- `B3` — the payment period, which is the first month in this example.
- `B4*12` — the total number of monthly payments.
- `B5` — the original loan amount.

### Step 3: Press Enter

Excel calculates the interest portion of the payment for the first month.

The result may appear as a negative number because it represents money paid out by the borrower.

For a standard loan with a fixed interest rate and equal monthly payments, the interest portion is generally highest at the beginning of the loan because the outstanding balance is largest.

## 5. Example 2: Calculating Interest for the Final Year

The lesson also demonstrates calculating interest for the final year of a loan with annual payments.

In this example, the loan has a three-year term, and you want to calculate the interest portion of the third and final payment.

If the annual interest rate is in cell `B2`, the total number of annual payments is in cell `B4`, and the loan amount is in cell `B5`, use:

```excel
=IPMT(B2, 3, B4, B5)
```

The arguments are:

- `B2` — the annual interest rate, because payments are annual.
- `3` — the third payment period.
- `B4` — the total number of annual payments.
- `B5` — the original loan amount.

**Important:** Unlike the first example, this calculation uses annual payments, so the interest rate does not need to be divided by 12 and the loan term does not need to be multiplied by 12.

Always match the interest rate and number of payment periods to the payment frequency.

## 6. Understanding Negative Results

IPMT commonly returns a negative value when the original loan amount is entered as a positive number.

This is because Excel treats the loan amount as money received and the payment as money paid out.

For example, a result of `-292` means approximately 292 units of currency are paid toward interest for that period.

The negative sign indicates the direction of the cash flow, not an error in the calculation.

## 7. Formatting the Result

You may want to display the interest amount with more decimal places.

To adjust the number of decimal places:

1. Select the cell containing the IPMT formula.
2. Go to the **Home** tab.
3. In the **Number** group, select **Increase Decimal** or **Decrease Decimal**.
4. Repeat until the result displays the desired precision.

You can also format the cell as currency if you want the interest payment to display as a monetary amount.

## 8. IPMT vs. PPMT vs. PMT

These three financial functions work together when building a loan schedule.

| Function | What It Calculates |
|---|---|
| `PMT` | The total regular payment for a loan. |
| `PPMT` | The principal portion of a payment for a specific period. |
| `IPMT` | The interest portion of a payment for a specific period. |

For a standard loan with equal payments:

**Total payment = Principal portion + Interest portion**

Therefore, the principal and interest portions can be used to understand how each payment is divided.

## 9. Important Things to Remember

- **IPMT calculates interest only.** It does not return the full loan payment.
- **The `per` argument identifies the payment period.** For example, `1` represents the first payment period, and `3` represents the third.
- **Match the interest rate to the payment frequency.** Divide the annual rate by 12 for monthly payments when the rate is quoted annually.
- **Calculate the total number of payments correctly.** Multiply the number of years by the number of payments per year.
- **Negative results are normal.** They represent money paid out toward interest.
- **Interest payments generally change over time.** For a standard fixed-rate loan with equal payments, the interest portion usually decreases as the outstanding balance is paid down.
- **The payment timing matters.** The optional `type` argument specifies whether payments occur at the beginning or end of each period.

## 10. Summary

The IPMT function calculates the interest portion of a loan payment for a particular period.

For monthly payments, the general formula is:

```excel
=IPMT(annual_rate/12, payment_period, years*12, loan_amount)
```

Using the worksheet cells from the first example:

```excel
=IPMT(B2/12, B3, B4*12, B5)
```

For annual payments, the formula uses the annual interest rate and the total number of annual payments directly.

**In summary:** IPMT is useful for identifying how much interest you pay during each period of a loan. When combined with PMT and PPMT, it helps you understand how loan payments are divided between interest and principal.
