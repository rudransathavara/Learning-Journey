# Forecasting Debt and Retained Earnings in Excel

## Overview

Debt and retained earnings connect the balance sheet to the income statement and cash flow statement.

This note explains how to forecast:

- Long-term debt
- Total liabilities
- Common stock
- Retained earnings
- Interest expense

## 1. Forecast Long-Term Debt

A simplified debt forecast is:

Closing Debt = Opening Debt + New Borrowings − Debt Repayments

Suppose:
- Opening debt is in `B21`.
- New borrowings are in `C42`.
- Debt repayments are in `C43`.
- Closing debt is calculated in `C21`.

Enter:

`=B21+C42-C43`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

### Example

Suppose:
- Opening debt is £2,000,000.
- New borrowings are £500,000.
- Debt repayments are £300,000.

Closing debt is:

£2,000,000 + £500,000 − £300,000 = £2,200,000

Make sure debt repayments are entered as positive amounts in this formula. If the repayment assumption is already negative, the formula must be adjusted to avoid adding the repayment back to debt.

## 2. Calculate Total Liabilities

Once the liability balances have been forecast, calculate total liabilities.

For example, if the relevant liability accounts are in cells `C17:C21`, enter:

`=SUM(C17:C21)`

Use the correct range for your workbook.

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 3. Forecast Common Stock

If the company is not expected to issue additional shares or change its common stock balance, the forecast may simply reference the opening balance.

For example, if opening common stock is in `B27`, enter in `C27`:

`=B27`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

If the company issues shares or buys them back, additional calculations will be required.

## 4. Forecast Retained Earnings

Retained earnings accumulate profits and losses that have not been distributed, adjusted for relevant movements.

A simplified formula is:

Closing Retained Earnings = Opening Retained Earnings + Net Income

Suppose:
- Opening retained earnings are in `B28`.
- Net income is in `B35` on the income statement.
- Closing retained earnings are calculated in `C28`.

Enter:

`=B28+'Income Statement'!B35`

Use the actual worksheet name in your workbook.

Copy the formula across the forecast years, ensuring that each year links to the correct net income.

If dividends or other retained earnings movements are included, adjust the formula accordingly.

## 5. Calculate Interest Expense

A simplified interest expense formula is:

Interest Expense = Debt Balance × Interest Rate

For example, if forecast debt is in `C21` and the interest rate is in `C44`, enter:

`=C21*C44`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

This is a simplified approach. More detailed models may calculate interest using average debt balances and account for the timing of borrowings and repayments.

## 6. Link Interest Expense to the Income Statement

If interest expense is calculated in cell `C45` on the balance sheet or debt schedule, link it to the interest expense line on the income statement.

For example:

`='Balance Sheet'!C45`

Use the actual worksheet name and cell reference from your workbook.

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

Check that the interest expense reduces earnings before tax in the income statement.

## 7. Understand the Balance Sheet Connection

These calculations help link the financial statements together:

- New borrowings increase debt and create a financing cash inflow.
- Debt repayments reduce debt and create a financing cash outflow.
- Interest expense reduces earnings before tax.
- Net income contributes to retained earnings.
- Retained earnings contribute to shareholders' equity.

The balance sheet may not balance until the cash flow statement has been completed and linked.

## Key Takeaways

- Debt forecasts should account for opening debt, new borrowing and repayments.
- Total liabilities are calculated by summing the liability accounts.
- Common stock can remain constant if no share transactions are expected.
- Retained earnings increase with net income, subject to dividends and other movements.
- Interest expense can be estimated from debt balances and interest rates.
- These calculations must be linked consistently across the three financial statements.
