# Calculating Income Tax in Excel

## Overview

Income tax expense can be forecast by applying an assumed tax rate to earnings before tax.

This is a simplified approach commonly used in introductory financial models.

## 1. Identify the Inputs

Suppose:

- Earnings before tax are in cell `B31`.
- The assumed tax rate is in cell `B45`.
- Tax expense is calculated in cell `B33`.

The assumed tax rate might be 22%.

## 2. Calculate Tax Expense

**Formula:**

Tax Expense = Earnings Before Tax × Assumed Tax Rate

Enter:

`=B31*B$45`

This multiplies earnings before tax by the assumed tax rate.

The mixed reference `B$45` fixes the assumption row while allowing the column to change when the formula is copied across.

## 3. Copy the Formula Across

Once the formula is correct:

1. Select the cell containing the formula.
2. Use the fill handle to copy it across the forecast years.
3. Check that each year references the appropriate earnings before tax and tax rate.

## 4. Format the Tax Rate

Format the tax rate assumption as a percentage.

For example:

- `0.22` displayed as `22%`
- `0.25` displayed as `25%`

Use consistent formatting throughout the assumptions section.

## 5. Important Modelling Considerations

A simple tax rate assumption may not reflect every real-world tax situation.

For example:

- A company may report a loss before tax.
- Tax losses from previous years may affect taxable profit.
- Interest expense may be subject to deductibility restrictions.
- Accounting profit may differ from taxable profit.
- Different types of income may be taxed differently.

For a basic training model, applying an assumed tax rate is a useful starting point. More advanced models may need separate tax calculations.

## Key Takeaways

- Tax expense can be forecast by multiplying earnings before tax by an assumed tax rate.
- Mixed references help maintain formulas when copying them across years.
- Tax assumptions should be clearly labelled and formatted as percentages.
- Actual tax calculations may require adjustments for losses, tax rules and differences between accounting and taxable profit.
