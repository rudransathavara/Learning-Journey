# Formatting and Conditional Formatting

## 1. Changing Font and Cell Colours

You can change the appearance of your data by adjusting the font colour and the background colour of cells.

- **Font Colour:** Select the cell or cells you want to change. Go to the **Home** ribbon and click **Font Colour** to change the colour of the text.
- **Cell Colour:** Select the cell or cells, then click **Fill Colour** in the **Home** ribbon to change the background colour.
- You can also select entire rows or columns to apply formatting to multiple cells at once.

## 2. Changing Row Height and Column Width

You can adjust the size of rows and columns to make your data easier to read.

- **Resize a Column:** Move your cursor to the right edge of the column letter at the top of the worksheet. When the cursor changes, click and drag to increase or decrease the column width.
- **Resize a Row:** Move your cursor to the bottom edge of the row number on the left side of the worksheet. Click and drag to change the row height.
- **Automatically Fit Content:** Double-click the boundary between two column letters or row numbers to automatically adjust the size to fit the contents.

## 3. Changing Text Alignment

You can control where text appears inside a cell.

1. Select the cell or range of cells you want to format.
2. Go to the **Home** ribbon.
3. Find the **Alignment** section.
4. Choose the alignment you want, such as:
   - **Align Left:** Places text on the left side of the cell.
   - **Centre:** Positions text in the middle of the cell horizontally.
   - **Align Right:** Places text on the right side of the cell.
   - **Top Align:** Positions content at the top of the cell.
   - **Middle Align:** Positions content in the middle of the cell vertically.
   - **Bottom Align:** Positions content at the bottom of the cell.

## 4. Changing Text Orientation

Excel allows you to rotate or tilt text inside cells.

1. Select the cell or cells you want to change.
2. Go to the **Home** ribbon.
3. Find the **Alignment** section.
4. Click **Orientation**.
5. Choose an option to rotate or angle the text.

This can be useful when working with narrow columns or long headings.

## 5. Formatting Numbers

Excel allows you to change how numbers are displayed without changing their underlying values.

1. Select the cell or cells you want to format.
2. Go to the **Home** ribbon.
3. Find the **Number** section.
4. Choose the format you need, such as:
   - **General:** Displays values using Excel's default format.
   - **Number:** Displays numeric values.
   - **Currency:** Displays values with a currency symbol, such as £.
   - **Percentage:** Displays values as percentages.
   - **Date:** Displays values as dates.
   - **Accounting:** Aligns currency symbols and decimal places consistently.

For example, formatting `0.25` as a percentage displays it as `25%`.

## 6. Merge and Centre

**Merge & Centre** combines multiple selected cells into one larger cell and centres the content.

To use it:

1. Select the cells you want to combine.
2. Go to the **Home** ribbon.
3. Find the **Alignment** section.
4. Click **Merge & Centre**.

This is useful for creating headings across several columns.

**Important:** When merging cells that contain data, Excel generally keeps the content from the upper-left cell and removes the contents of the other cells. Make sure you do not need that other data before merging.

## 7. What Is Conditional Formatting?

Conditional Formatting automatically changes the appearance of cells when they meet conditions you specify.

It is useful for highlighting important values, identifying patterns, and making data easier to understand.

### Highlighting Cells Based on Values

You can highlight cells that contain values greater than, less than, equal to, or greater than or equal to a particular number.

To do this:

1. Select the cells you want to format.
2. Go to the **Home** ribbon.
3. Click **Conditional Formatting** in the **Styles** section.
4. Select **Highlight Cells Rules**.
5. Choose a condition, such as:
   - Greater Than
   - Less Than
   - Equal To
   - Between
6. Enter the value you want Excel to check.
7. Choose a formatting style or colour.
8. Click **OK**.

For example, if you have a list of sales figures, you can highlight every value greater than £1,000.

### Using Data Bars

Data Bars display horizontal bars inside cells. The length of each bar depends on the value in the cell.

To apply Data Bars:

1. Select the cells containing your numbers.
2. Go to the **Home** ribbon.
3. Click **Conditional Formatting**.
4. Select **Data Bars**.
5. Choose a style or colour.

Larger values generally display longer bars, making it easier to compare numbers visually.

### Managing Conditional Formatting Rules

You can edit or remove existing rules whenever you need to change how your data is highlighted.

1. Select the cells affected by the rules.
2. Go to the **Home** ribbon.
3. Click **Conditional Formatting**.
4. Select **Manage Rules** at the bottom of the menu.
5. Select the rule you want to change.
6. Click **Edit Rule** to adjust its conditions or formatting.
7. Click **OK** or **Apply** to save your changes.

You can also use this menu to delete rules or change which cells they apply to.

## Key Takeaways

- You can change font colours and cell background colours using the Home ribbon.
- You can resize rows and columns by dragging their boundaries.
- You can align, rotate, and format text inside cells.
- You can format numbers as currency, percentages, dates, and other types.
- Merge & Centre combines selected cells and centres their content.
- Conditional Formatting automatically highlights cells based on rules.
- Data Bars help you compare values visually.
- Manage Rules allows you to edit or remove conditional formatting rules.
