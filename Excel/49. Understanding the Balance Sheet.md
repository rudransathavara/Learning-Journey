# Understanding the Balance Sheet

## Overview

A balance sheet shows a company's financial position at a particular point in time.

It reports what the business owns, what it owes and the residual interest belonging to its owners.

The balance sheet is one of the three core financial statements.

## 1. Assets

Assets are resources controlled by the business that are expected to provide economic benefits.

### Current Assets

Current assets are generally expected to be realised, sold or consumed within the normal operating cycle or within a year.

Examples include:

- Cash
- Accounts receivable
- Inventory
- Other short-term assets

### Cash

Cash represents money held by the business.

In a three-statement model, forecast cash is linked to the cash flow statement.

### Accounts Receivable

Accounts receivable represents amounts owed to the business by customers.

For example, if a company issues an invoice but has not yet received payment, the outstanding amount may be recorded as accounts receivable.

### Fixed Assets

Fixed assets are long-term assets used in business operations.

Examples include:
- Equipment
- Machinery
- Computers
- Buildings

Gross fixed assets are reduced by accumulated depreciation to calculate net fixed assets.

### Accumulated Depreciation

Accumulated depreciation represents the depreciation recognised on an asset since it was placed in service.

It is generally shown as a deduction from gross fixed assets.

### Total Assets

Total assets are calculated by adding the relevant asset balances.

For example, if the assets are in cells `B5:B12`, a suitable total might be:

`=SUM(B5:B12)`

Use the range that matches the actual asset rows in the workbook.

## 2. Liabilities

Liabilities are obligations the business owes to other parties.

### Accounts Payable

Accounts payable represents amounts the business owes to suppliers.

These amounts commonly arise when a business receives goods or services before paying for them.

### Deferred Revenue

Deferred revenue arises when a business receives payment before delivering the relevant goods or services.

For example, a customer may pay in advance for a service that will be delivered in a future period.

The payment is generally recorded as a liability until the relevant revenue recognition requirements are met.

### Long-Term Debt

Long-term debt includes borrowing obligations that extend beyond the short term.

Examples include:
- Bank loans
- Corporate loans
- Other long-term borrowings

### Total Liabilities

Total liabilities equal the sum of the relevant liability balances.

For example:

`=SUM(B16:B21)`

## 3. Shareholders' Equity

Shareholders' equity represents the residual interest in the company's assets after deducting liabilities.

### Common Stock

Common stock represents the share capital recorded when a company issues shares, subject to its legal and accounting structure.

### Retained Earnings

Retained earnings represent accumulated profits and losses that have not been distributed, adjusted for relevant movements such as dividends.

A simplified forecast is:

Closing Retained Earnings = Opening Retained Earnings + Net Income

For example:

`=B28+'Income Statement'!B35`

This assumes opening retained earnings are in `B28` and net income is in `B35` on the income statement.

The actual formula should also account for dividends or other relevant movements where applicable.

### Total Shareholders' Equity

Total shareholders' equity is the sum of the relevant equity balances.

For example:

`=SUM(B27:B28)`

Use the appropriate range for the workbook.

## 4. The Accounting Equation

The balance sheet follows the accounting equation:

**Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity**

This means that total assets must equal total liabilities plus total shareholders' equity.

If the two sides do not agree, the model contains an error or an incomplete calculation that needs to be investigated.

## 5. Calculate a Balance Check

A balance check helps confirm that the accounting equation holds.

**Formula:**

Balance Check = Total Assets − Total Liabilities and Shareholders' Equity

For example, if:
- Total assets are in `B13`.
- Total liabilities and shareholders' equity are in `B31`.

Enter:

`=B13-B31`

A result of zero means the two sides balance.

A non-zero result indicates that something needs to be investigated.

## Key Takeaways

- The balance sheet shows a company's financial position at a point in time.
- Assets represent resources controlled by the business.
- Liabilities represent obligations owed to other parties.
- Shareholders' equity represents the residual interest in the business.
- Assets must equal liabilities plus shareholders' equity.
- A balance check helps identify incomplete or incorrect model calculations.
