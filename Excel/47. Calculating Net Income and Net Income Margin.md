# Calculating Net Income and Net Income Margin in Excel

## Overview

Net income is the profit remaining after deducting income tax from earnings before tax.

Net income margin measures the proportion of net revenue that remains as net income.

Both are important measures of a company's profitability.

## 1. Calculate Net Income

**Formula:**

Net Income = Earnings Before Tax − Tax Expense

Suppose:

- Earnings before tax are in `B31`.
- Tax expense is in `B33`.
- Net income is calculated in `B35`.

Enter:

`=B31-B33`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 2. Calculate Net Income Margin

**Formula:**

Net Income Margin = Net Income / Net Revenue

If net income is in `B35` and net revenue is in `B9`, enter:

`=B35/B9`

Format the result as a percentage.

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 3. Example

Suppose a company has:

- Net revenue: £1,000,000
- Net income: £100,000

The net income margin is:

£100,000 / £1,000,000 = 10%

The company generates £0.10 of net income for every £1 of net revenue.

## 4. Link Net Income to Retained Earnings

Net income is an important link between the income statement and the balance sheet.

Retained earnings generally accumulate profits and losses over time, adjusted for items such as dividends and other relevant movements.

A simplified forecast formula is:

Closing Retained Earnings = Opening Retained Earnings + Net Income

For example, if opening retained earnings are in `B28` and net income is in `B35` on the income statement, the first forecast year's retained earnings could be calculated as:

`=B28+'Income Statement'!B35`

Use the actual worksheet name in your workbook.

If dividends or other retained earnings movements are included, the formula needs to account for them as well.

## 5. Formatting

- Use bold formatting for net income.
- Format net income margin as a percentage.
- Use consistent number formats across forecast years.
- Apply borders where appropriate to distinguish totals.
- Check that net income is linked correctly to retained earnings.

## Key Takeaways

- Net income equals earnings before tax minus tax expense.
- Net income margin equals net income divided by net revenue.
- Net income margin helps assess how much revenue remains as profit.
- Net income is an important link between the income statement and balance sheet.
