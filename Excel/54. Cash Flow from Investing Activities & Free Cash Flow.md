# Cash Flow from Investing Activities and Free Cash Flow

## Overview

Investing activities include cash spent on or received from long-term investments and assets.

For many businesses, capital expenditure is an important investing cash flow.

Free cash flow is a useful measure of the cash remaining after capital expenditure, although its exact definition can vary.

## 1. Link Capital Expenditure

Capital expenditure represents investment in long-term assets.

Examples include:
- Equipment
- Machinery
- Computers
- Eligible software development costs

CapEx is generally presented as a negative amount in the cash flow statement because it represents a cash outflow.

If CapEx is recorded as a positive amount in cell `C7` on the CapEx and depreciation schedule, link it to the cash flow statement and apply a negative sign.

For example:

`=-'CapEx and Depreciation Schedule'!C7`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

This assumes the CapEx schedule stores expenditure as a positive amount.

If CapEx is already stored as a negative amount, do not apply another negative sign.

## 2. Calculate Free Cash Flow

A simplified definition of free cash flow is:

Free Cash Flow = Cash Flow from Operating Activities − Capital Expenditure

If CapEx is recorded as a positive amount, the formula is:

`=B10-B13`

This assumes:
- Operating cash flow is in `B10`.
- CapEx is in `B13` and is stored as a positive number.

If CapEx is already recorded as a negative amount in the cash flow statement, use:

`=B10+B13`

Choose the formula that matches the sign convention used in the workbook.

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 3. Understand Free Cash Flow

Free cash flow indicates how much cash remains after operating activities and capital expenditure.

It can be used to assess whether a business is generating cash that may be available for purposes such as:

- Debt repayment
- Dividends
- Additional investment
- Building cash reserves

Free cash flow may be positive or negative.

A negative figure is not automatically a sign of poor performance. A growing business may spend heavily on assets or expansion.

## 4. Formatting

- Present CapEx as a cash outflow.
- Use consistent number formats.
- Apply borders to distinguish free cash flow from the lines above it.
- Check that the CapEx sign convention is consistent throughout the model.

## Key Takeaways

- CapEx is generally an investing cash outflow.
- Free cash flow is commonly calculated as operating cash flow less CapEx.
- The formula depends on whether CapEx is stored as a positive or negative amount.
- Free cash flow helps assess cash available after operating needs and capital investment.
