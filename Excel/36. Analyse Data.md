# Analyse Data in Excel

## Overview

**Analyse Data** is a feature in Microsoft Excel that helps users explore datasets by suggesting questions, PivotTables, charts and other insights.

It is particularly useful when you are unsure where to start with a dataset or want to identify patterns without manually building every analysis from scratch.

The feature is available in supported versions of Microsoft 365 and Excel desktop. Its availability and functionality may vary depending on your Excel version.

## Why Use Analyse Data?

- Automatically suggests PivotTables and charts based on your data.
- Helps identify patterns and trends.
- Allows you to ask questions about your dataset using natural language.
- Can insert suggested analyses directly into your workbook.
- Reduces the time needed to explore data manually.

## How to Use Analyse Data

### Step 1: Select Your Data

1. Open your Excel workbook.
2. Select the dataset you want to analyse.
3. Make sure your data has clear column headings.

**Example:** In the practice file `Analyse Table`, select the range `A1:H19`.

### Step 2: Open Analyse Data

1. Go to the **Home** tab.
2. Find the **Analyse** group, if available in your version.
3. Click **Analyse Data**.

Excel opens a pane containing suggested analyses based on your selected data.

> **Note:** The exact location and wording of this feature may vary between Excel versions. If the feature is labelled differently in your version, use the name displayed in your Excel interface.

### Step 3: Explore Suggested Insights

The Analyse Data pane may suggest:

- PivotTables
- Charts
- Summaries of numerical values
- Relationships and patterns in your data
- Questions you can ask about the dataset

Scroll through the suggestions to find an analysis that could be useful.

The number and type of suggestions depend on your data and Excel version.

### Step 4: Ask Your Own Question

You can type a question into the search box.

For example:

`Average of income`

Press **Enter** to see the result.

Excel attempts to interpret your question and generate a relevant summary or visualisation.

### Step 5: Insert a PivotTable

If Excel suggests a useful PivotTable:

1. Find the relevant suggestion.
2. Click **Insert PivotTable**.
3. Excel inserts the PivotTable, typically on a new worksheet.
4. Review the results to make sure they answer your question.

### Step 6: Insert a Chart

To use a suggested chart:

1. Find a chart suggestion in the Analyse Data pane.
2. Review the visualisation.
3. Click **Insert Chart**.
4. Excel adds the chart to your workbook.

You can then customise the chart using Excel's chart formatting tools.

### Step 7: Close or Move the Pane

When you finish analysing your data:

- Use the pane's close button (**X**) to close it.
- If available, use the pane controls to move or reposition it.

## Important Tips

- **Check the results:** Automatically generated insights are suggestions, not guaranteed correct conclusions.
- **Use clear headings:** Descriptive column names help Excel interpret your data.
- **Keep your data organised:** Consistent data types and a clean table improve the usefulness of the suggestions.
- **Ask specific questions:** Clear questions can produce more relevant results.
- **Verify calculations:** Confirm that the suggested summary uses the correct fields and calculation, such as Sum or Average.

## Key Takeaways

- Analyse Data helps you explore datasets quickly.
- It can suggest PivotTables, charts and other insights.
- You can ask questions in natural language.
- Suggested analyses can be inserted directly into your workbook.
- Always review the results before using them in a report or business decision.
