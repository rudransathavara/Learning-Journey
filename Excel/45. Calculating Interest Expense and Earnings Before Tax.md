# Calculating Interest Expense and Earnings Before Tax in Excel

## Overview

Interest expense represents the cost of borrowing money.

Earnings before tax (EBT) measures profit after interest expense but before income tax.

In a three-statement model, interest expense is generally calculated using the debt schedule and linked into the income statement.

## 1. Understand Interest Expense

Interest expense can arise from:

- Bank loans
- Corporate debt
- Other interest-bearing borrowings

The amount depends on the debt outstanding and the applicable interest rate.

If the interest expense has not yet been calculated, flag the relevant cell as incomplete rather than assuming that the expense is zero.

## 2. Calculate Interest Payments

A simplified calculation is:

Interest Expense = Debt Balance × Interest Rate

For example, if debt is £1,000,000 and the annual interest rate is 7%:

£1,000,000 × 7% = £70,000

In a financial model, the debt balance and interest rate should reference the appropriate cells in the debt schedule.

The calculation may need to account for the timing of new borrowings and repayments.

## 3. Calculate Earnings Before Tax

**Formula:**

Earnings Before Tax = Operating Income − Interest Expense

If operating income is in `B27` and interest expense is in `B29`, enter:

`=B27-B29`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 4. Understand the Tax Effect of Interest

Interest expense reduces accounting profit before tax.

Depending on the applicable tax rules, deductible interest can also reduce taxable profit.

However, interest deductibility is subject to jurisdiction-specific rules and limitations.

Taking on additional debt also increases repayment obligations and financial risk.

## 5. Formatting

- Use consistent number formats for interest expense.
- Keep interest rate assumptions formatted as percentages.
- Use temporary highlighting for incomplete debt calculations.
- Remove highlighting once the debt schedule has been linked.

## Key Takeaways

- Interest expense represents the cost of borrowing.
- Interest can be estimated using debt balances and interest rates.
- Earnings before tax equals operating income minus interest expense.
- Interest expense affects profit and may affect taxable income.
- Debt assumptions should be linked consistently across the model.
