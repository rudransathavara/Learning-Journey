# Checking and Troubleshooting a Three-Statement Financial Model

## Overview

A three-statement model links the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement.

Once the calculations are complete, it is important to check that the statements are linked correctly and that the balance sheet balances.

## 1. Check the Balance Sheet

The accounting equation is:

Assets = Liabilities + Shareholders' Equity

A balance check can be calculated as:

Balance Check = Total Assets − Total Liabilities and Shareholders' Equity

For example, if:
- Total assets are in `C13`.
- Total liabilities and shareholders' equity are in `C31`.

Enter:

`=C13-C31`

A result of zero means the balance sheet balances.

A non-zero result indicates that the model needs further investigation.

## 2. Check That Net Income Links to Retained Earnings

Net income from the income statement should flow into retained earnings on the balance sheet.

A simplified forecast formula is:

Closing Retained Earnings = Opening Retained Earnings + Net Income

If net income is not linked correctly, the balance sheet may not balance.

Remember to account for dividends or other relevant retained earnings movements if they are included in the model.

## 3. Check That Cash Links to the Cash Flow Statement

The balance sheet cash balance should reflect the movement in cash calculated by the cash flow statement.

A simplified formula is:

Closing Cash = Opening Cash + Net Cash Flow

Check that:
- The correct year's net cash flow is linked.
- Each forecast year uses the previous year's closing cash as its opening cash.
- Cash flow signs are consistent.

## 4. Check All Balance Sheet Accounts

Every balance sheet account that affects cash flow should be considered in the cash flow statement where appropriate.

Common examples include:
- Accounts receivable
- Accounts payable
- Inventory
- Deferred revenue
- Other operating working capital accounts
- Capital expenditure
- Debt repayments
- New borrowings

If a relevant account is omitted, the balance sheet may not balance.

The appropriate cash flow adjustments depend on the nature of the account and the company's activities.

## 5. Check the Historical Financial Statements

Historical financial statements should also be checked.

If the historical balance sheet does not balance, the forecast may inherit the error.

Review the opening balances and confirm that historical assets, liabilities and equity are consistent.

## 6. Check Formula References

Common spreadsheet errors include:

- Incorrect cell references
- Incorrect signs on cash inflows and outflows
- Missing working capital accounts
- Incorrect absolute or mixed references
- Missing depreciation or interest expense
- Formulas copied across the wrong rows or columns
- Incorrect links between worksheets

Review formulas carefully when a result does not look reasonable.

## 7. Remove Temporary Formatting

During model construction, highlighting can help identify incomplete calculations.

Before finalising the model:

- Remove temporary highlighting from completed cells.
- Confirm that number formats are consistent.
- Check that percentages are formatted correctly.
- Use borders consistently for subtotals.
- Remove temporary reminders that are no longer needed.

## Final Checklist

Before considering the model complete, confirm that:

- [ ] The income statement formulas are complete.
- [ ] Depreciation and amortisation are linked correctly.
- [ ] Interest expense is linked from the debt schedule.
- [ ] Net income flows into retained earnings.
- [ ] CapEx flows into the balance sheet and cash flow statement.
- [ ] Relevant working capital movements are included in cash flow.
- [ ] Debt repayments and new borrowings are reflected correctly.
- [ ] Cash is linked to net cash flow.
- [ ] The balance check is zero for every forecast year.
- [ ] Temporary highlighting has been removed.

## Key Takeaways

- The three financial statements must be linked consistently.
- Net income, retained earnings and cash are particularly important connections.
- Every relevant balance sheet movement should be reflected in the model.
- A zero balance check is an essential validation step.
- A model should be checked for both formula accuracy and presentation consistency.
