# Forecasting the Balance Sheet: Working Capital

## Overview

A balance sheet forecast estimates how a company's financial position will change over time.

This note focuses on forecasting:

- Net revenue as a supporting assumption
- Accounts receivable
- Accounts payable
- Deferred revenue

These items can be forecast using assumptions expressed as a percentage of net revenue.

## 1. Link Net Revenue from the Income Statement

The balance sheet forecast often uses net revenue from the income statement as a supporting assumption.

Suppose the first forecast year's net revenue is in `B9` on the income statement.

In the first forecast cell of the balance sheet assumptions, enter:

`='Income Statement'!B9`

Use the actual worksheet name in your workbook.

Copy the formula across the forecast years so that each year links to the corresponding income statement value.

## 2. Forecast Accounts Receivable

Accounts receivable represents amounts customers owe the business.

A simplified forecast formula is:

Accounts Receivable = Net Revenue × Accounts Receivable % of Revenue

Suppose:
- Net revenue is in `C37`.
- The accounts receivable assumption is in `C38`.
- Accounts receivable is calculated in `C6`.

Enter:

`=C37*C38`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

### Example

If net revenue is £1,000,000 and accounts receivable is forecast at 10% of net revenue:

£1,000,000 × 10% = £100,000

The forecast accounts receivable balance is £100,000.

## 3. Calculate Total Current Assets

Once the relevant current asset balances have been calculated, total them.

For example, if cash is in `C5` and accounts receivable is in `C6`, enter:

`=SUM(C5:C6)`

Adjust the formula if the workbook includes additional current asset categories.

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 4. Forecast Accounts Payable

Accounts payable represents amounts owed to suppliers.

A simplified forecast formula is:

Accounts Payable = Net Revenue × Accounts Payable % of Revenue

Suppose:
- Net revenue is in `C37`.
- The accounts payable assumption is in `C39`.
- Accounts payable is calculated in `C17`.

Enter:

`=C37*C39`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 5. Forecast Deferred Revenue

Deferred revenue represents amounts received before the business has delivered the relevant goods or services.

A simplified forecast formula is:

Deferred Revenue = Net Revenue × Deferred Revenue % of Revenue

Suppose:
- Net revenue is in `C37`.
- The deferred revenue assumption is in `C40`.
- Deferred revenue is calculated in `C18`.

Enter:

`=C37*C40`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 6. Formatting the Forecast

- Use consistent number formats across all forecast years.
- Keep assumptions clearly labelled.
- Apply borders to distinguish subtotals from individual accounts.
- Use percentage formatting for the assumptions.
- Check that all formulas reference the correct forecast year.

## Key Takeaways

- Working capital accounts can be forecast using revenue-based assumptions.
- Accounts receivable represents amounts customers owe the business.
- Accounts payable represents amounts owed to suppliers.
- Deferred revenue represents payments received before the relevant goods or services are delivered.
- Revenue-based assumptions are useful for introductory models, but more detailed models may use operating drivers such as collection days or payment terms.
