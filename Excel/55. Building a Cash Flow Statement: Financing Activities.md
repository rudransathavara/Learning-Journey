# Building a Cash Flow Statement: Financing Activities

## Overview

Financing activities show how a business raises capital and repays financing.

Common items include:
- Debt repayments
- New borrowings
- Share issues
- Dividends

This note focuses on debt repayments, new borrowings and net cash flow from financing.

## 1. Calculate Debt Repayments

Debt repayments are cash outflows and should generally appear as negative amounts in the cash flow statement.

If the debt repayment assumption is stored as a positive amount in cell `C43` on the balance sheet, enter:

`=-C43`

This converts the repayment into a negative cash flow.

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

If repayments are already stored as negative values, do not reverse their sign again.

## 2. Calculate New Borrowings

New borrowings represent cash received when a business takes on additional debt.

They are generally presented as positive financing cash flows.

If new borrowing is recorded in cell `C42` on the balance sheet, enter:

`=C42`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 3. Calculate Net Cash Flow from Financing

Net cash flow from financing is the sum of the financing cash flows included in the model.

If:
- Debt repayments are in `B18`.
- New borrowings are in `B19`.

Enter:

`=SUM(B18:B19)`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

A positive result means financing activities generated net cash.

A negative result means financing activities used net cash.

## 4. Calculate Net Cash Flow

A simplified net cash flow calculation is:

Net Cash Flow = Free Cash Flow + Net Cash Flow from Financing

If:
- Free cash flow is in `B15`.
- Net cash flow from financing is in `B20`.

Enter:

`=SUM(B15,B20)`

This simplified formula assumes that free cash flow already represents operating cash flow less capital expenditure and that all other relevant investing and financing items have been included.

If the model contains additional cash flow categories, include them as appropriate.

## 5. Link Net Cash Flow to the Balance Sheet

The closing cash balance on the balance sheet should reflect the movement in cash during the period.

**Formula:**

Closing Cash = Opening Cash + Net Cash Flow

For example, if:
- Opening cash is in `B5`.
- Net cash flow is in `B22`.
- Closing cash is calculated in `C5`.

Enter:

`=B5+'Cash Flow Statement'!B22`

Use the actual worksheet name and cell references in your workbook.

Copy the formula across the forecast years, making sure each year's closing cash becomes the next year's opening cash.

## Key Takeaways

- Debt repayments are financing cash outflows.
- New borrowings are financing cash inflows.
- Net cash flow from financing is the sum of financing cash flows.
- Net cash flow combines the relevant operating, investing and financing movements.
- Closing cash links the cash flow statement to the balance sheet.
