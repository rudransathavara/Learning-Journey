# Purchase Price Variance (PPV) in Excel

## Overview

**Purchase Price Variance (PPV)** measures the difference between the price paid for a good or service and its standard or expected price.

Businesses use purchase price variance analysis to monitor purchasing costs, evaluate supplier pricing, manage inventory costs and understand how purchasing decisions affect profitability.

PPV is commonly used in finance, accounting, procurement and inventory management.

## Why Is PPV Important?

Purchase price variance can help an organisation:

- Monitor actual purchasing costs against budgeted or standard prices.
- Identify cost increases or potential savings.
- Evaluate supplier pricing.
- Improve purchasing decisions.
- Understand the effect of purchase costs on profitability.
- Support inventory and cost management.

## Understanding the Main Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Standard Price | The expected or budgeted price per unit. |
| Actual Price | The price actually paid per unit. |
| Quantity | The number of units purchased. |
| Price Variance | The difference between actual and standard pricing. |
| Purchase Price Variance | The monetary difference between actual and standard purchase costs, calculated using the applicable accounting convention. |

## Common PPV Calculations

There are several ways to express purchase price variance. The correct formula depends on whether you want the total monetary difference, the per-unit difference or a percentage.

### 1. Total Monetary Purchase Price Variance

A common formula is:

**PPV = (Actual Price − Standard Price) × Quantity**

This calculates the total difference between the actual purchase cost and the standard purchase cost.

#### Example

Suppose a company has the following figures:

- Standard price per unit: £10
- Actual price per unit: £12
- Quantity purchased: 100 units

Calculation:

`(£12 − £10) × 100 = £200`

The actual purchase cost is £200 higher than the standard cost.

Using the convention above, the positive result represents an unfavourable variance because the company paid more than expected.

Now suppose the actual price was £9:

`(£9 − £10) × 100 = -£100`

The negative result represents a favourable variance because the company paid £100 less than the standard cost.

**Important:** Accounting systems may use different sign conventions. Always confirm how your organisation defines favourable and unfavourable variances.

### 2. Percentage Price Variance

A common way to express the price difference as a percentage of the standard price is:

**Percentage Price Variance = (Actual Price − Standard Price) ÷ Standard Price**

Format the result as a percentage in Excel.

#### Example

Suppose:

- Standard price: £10
- Actual price: £12

Calculation:

`(£12 − £10) ÷ £10 = 0.20 = 20%`

The actual price is 20% higher than the standard price.

If the actual price is £9:

`(£9 − £10) ÷ £10 = -0.10 = -10%`

The actual price is 10% lower than the standard price.

This percentage formula measures the price difference relative to the standard price. It does not calculate the total monetary PPV.

## Calculating PPV in Excel

The following examples assume your worksheet has separate columns for standard price, actual price and quantity.

### Option A: Calculate Total PPV

Suppose:

- Column B contains Standard Price.
- Column C contains Actual Price.
- Column D contains Quantity.

In cell E3, enter:

`=(C3-B3)*D3`

Press **Enter**.

This calculates the total monetary variance for that row.

### Option B: Calculate Percentage Price Variance

Using the same example column layout, enter:

`=(C3-B3)/B3`

Press **Enter**.

Then:

1. Select the formula cell.
2. Go to the **Home** tab.
3. Click **Percent Style (%)**.
4. Adjust the number of decimal places if necessary.

**Note:** The cell references above are illustrative. Match them to the actual column layout in your workbook.

### Step 3: Copy the Formula Down

To apply the formula to additional rows:

1. Select the cell containing the formula.
2. Move the pointer to the small square at the bottom-right corner of the cell.
3. Drag the fill handle down through the remaining rows.

Excel adjusts the relative cell references for each row.

### Step 4: Review the Results

Check that:

- The standard and actual prices are in the correct columns.
- The quantity is used when calculating total monetary variance.
- The percentage formula divides by the standard price, not the quantity.
- The results have the appropriate number or percentage format.
- Positive and negative values are interpreted using the intended sign convention.

## Understanding Positive and Negative Results

If you use the formula:

`Actual Price − Standard Price`

then:

| Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Positive | Actual price is higher than standard price. Usually unfavourable. |
| Negative | Actual price is lower than standard price. Usually favourable. |
| Zero | Actual price equals standard price. No price variance. |

The interpretation changes if the formula is reversed.

For example, if you use `Standard Price − Actual Price`, a positive result indicates that the actual price is lower than the standard price.

**Always check the formula before deciding whether a positive variance represents savings.**

## Important Formula Distinctions

Do not confuse these calculations:

| Calculation | Formula | What It Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Per-unit price difference | Actual Price − Standard Price | Difference in price for one unit |
| Total monetary PPV | (Actual Price − Standard Price) × Quantity | Total cost difference for the quantity purchased |
| Percentage price variance | (Actual Price − Standard Price) ÷ Standard Price | Price difference as a percentage of the standard price |

Dividing the price difference by quantity does not produce the standard total monetary PPV or the usual percentage price variance. Use that calculation only if your workbook specifically defines a different measure.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Using the wrong formula:** Confirm whether you need a per-unit difference, total variance or percentage variance.
- **Using the wrong sign convention:** A positive value does not automatically mean the company saved money.
- **Dividing by quantity when calculating a percentage:** The usual percentage price variance uses the standard price as the denominator.
- **Forgetting percentage formatting:** Excel may display a decimal such as `0.20` instead of `20%`.
- **Using incorrect cell references:** Check that the formula refers to the correct standard price, actual price and quantity.
- **Ignoring the business context:** A lower purchase price may not always mean a better outcome if quality, shipping costs or other purchasing conditions differ.

## Key Takeaways

- Purchase price variance compares actual purchasing prices with standard prices.
- Total monetary PPV commonly uses the price difference multiplied by quantity.
- Percentage price variance expresses the price difference relative to the standard price.
- Excel formulas can calculate these measures and be copied down a table.
- Favourable and unfavourable results depend on the formula and accounting convention used.
- PPV helps businesses monitor purchasing costs and understand their impact on profitability.
