# Building a CapEx and Depreciation Schedule in Excel

## Overview

A capital expenditure (CapEx) and depreciation schedule tracks investment in long-term assets and allocates their cost over their useful lives.

It connects the following parts of a three-statement financial model:

- Capital expenditure
- Depreciation and amortisation
- Income statement
- Balance sheet
- Cash flow statement

## 1. Understand the Schedule

A typical schedule includes:

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Asset | The asset being purchased or tracked |
| Useful life | The number of years the asset is expected to be used |
| Capital expenditure | The amount spent acquiring or developing an asset |
| Depreciation or amortisation | The portion of an asset's cost recognised as an expense |
| Total depreciation and amortisation | The combined expense for the period |

Examples of assets include laptops, machinery, ovens and eligible software assets.

## 2. Understand Depreciation

Depreciation allocates the cost of a tangible asset over its useful life.

For example, if a company buys equipment for £50,000 and expects to use it for five years, straight-line depreciation would be:

Annual Depreciation = £50,000 / 5 = £10,000

This assumes no residual value and five full years of depreciation.

Actual depreciation depends on the asset's useful life, residual value, accounting policy and when the asset is available for use.

## 3. Calculate Straight-Line Depreciation

Suppose:

- Asset cost is in `C4`.
- Useful life is in `B4`.
- Annual depreciation is calculated in `C10`.

Enter:

`=$C$4/$B$4`

The dollar signs lock both the column and row references.

Copy the formula across the forecast years where the asset is in use.

Press **F4** while editing a reference to cycle through Excel's reference types.

## 4. Calculate Depreciation for Multiple Asset Purchases

A business may purchase additional assets in different years.

Each purchase should be tracked separately so that depreciation begins in the appropriate period and continues over the asset's useful life.

For example, if purchases occur in cells `C5`, `D5` and `E5`, with useful life in `B5`, each purchase's annual depreciation can be calculated separately.

For a purchase recorded in `C5`:

`=$C$5/$B$5`

For a purchase recorded in `D5`:

`=$D$5/$B$5`

The total depreciation for a year should include only the purchases that are being depreciated in that year.

Check that:
- Each purchase is linked to the correct year.
- Useful-life assumptions remain fixed where appropriate.
- Depreciation stops when the asset reaches the end of its depreciable life.
- Asset costs are not depreciated more than once.

## 5. Calculate Depreciation for New Assets

Suppose new equipment costs £100,000 and has a useful life of five years.

If the cost is in `E6` and the useful life is in `B6`, enter:

`=$E$6/$B$6`

This calculates annual straight-line depreciation.

If the asset is purchased in a later year, depreciation should begin in the appropriate period under the company's accounting policy.

## 6. Calculate Total Depreciation and Amortisation

Once the individual asset calculations are complete, add them together.

If the relevant depreciation amounts are in cells `C10:C12`, enter in `C13`:

`=SUM(C10:C12)`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 7. Link Depreciation to the Income Statement

Depreciation and amortisation should flow into the income statement as expenses.

If total depreciation and amortisation for the first forecast year is in `C13` on the CapEx and depreciation schedule, link it to the income statement.

For example:

`='CapEx and Depreciation Schedule'!C13`

Use the actual worksheet name in your workbook.

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 8. Link CapEx to the Balance Sheet

Capital expenditure represents investment in long-term assets.

A simplified calculation is:

Closing Gross Fixed Assets = Opening Gross Fixed Assets + CapEx

If opening gross fixed assets are in `B9` on the balance sheet and the first year's CapEx is in `C7` on the CapEx schedule, enter:

`=B9+'CapEx and Depreciation Schedule'!C7`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

Additional adjustments may be required for disposals, impairments or other asset movements.

## 9. Calculate Accumulated Depreciation

Accumulated depreciation represents depreciation recognised on an asset since it was placed in service.

It is generally shown as a deduction from gross fixed assets.

If accumulated depreciation is represented as a negative balance, a simplified forecast is:

Closing Accumulated Depreciation = Opening Accumulated Depreciation − Current-Year Depreciation

For example:

`=B10-'CapEx and Depreciation Schedule'!C13`

This assumes the opening accumulated depreciation is in `B10` and current-year depreciation and amortisation is in `C13`.

## 10. Calculate Net Fixed Assets

Net fixed assets equal gross fixed assets less accumulated depreciation.

If accumulated depreciation is stored as a negative value, and gross fixed assets and accumulated depreciation are in rows 9 and 10, enter:

`=SUM(B9:B10)`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 11. Understand the Cash Flow Impact

CapEx appears as an investing cash outflow in the cash flow statement.

Depreciation and amortisation are non-cash expenses. Under the indirect method, they are added back to net income when calculating cash flow from operating activities.

This is why CapEx and depreciation must be modelled separately.

## Key Takeaways

- CapEx tracks investment in long-term assets.
- Depreciation allocates the cost of tangible assets over their useful lives.
- Amortisation applies a similar approach to eligible intangible assets.
- Total depreciation and amortisation should be linked to the income statement.
- CapEx increases gross fixed assets and appears as an investing cash outflow.
- Accumulated depreciation reduces the carrying value of fixed assets.
