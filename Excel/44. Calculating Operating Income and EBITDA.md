# Calculating Operating Income and EBITDA in Excel

## Overview

Operating income and EBITDA are important measures of business performance.

Operating income measures profit after operating expenses, while EBITDA adds depreciation and amortisation back to operating income.

## 1. Calculate Operating Income

Operating income measures the profit generated by the business's operations after operating expenses.

**Formula:**

Operating Income = Gross Profit − Total Operating Expenses

If:

- Gross profit is in `B17`.
- Total operating expenses are in `B25`.

Enter:

`=B17-B25`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

Operating income is also commonly referred to as operating profit.

## 2. Understand Depreciation and Amortisation

Depreciation and amortisation are expenses recognised over the useful lives of eligible assets.

They reduce accounting profit but do not normally represent cash payments in the period in which the expense is recognised.

The amounts should be calculated in the capital expenditure and depreciation schedule and linked into the income statement.

If depreciation has not yet been calculated, operating income will be incomplete.

## 3. Calculate EBITDA

EBITDA stands for:

**Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortisation**

A simplified formula is:

EBITDA = Operating Income + Depreciation and Amortisation

If operating income is in `B27` and depreciation and amortisation are in `B24`, enter:

`=B27+B24`

Copy the formula across the forecast years.

## 4. Why EBITDA Matters

EBITDA is widely used in financial analysis, company valuation and private equity.

It allows analysts to compare operating performance before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation.

However, EBITDA is not the same as cash flow.

It does not account for items such as:

- Capital expenditure
- Changes in working capital
- Interest payments
- Tax payments
- Debt repayments

These items are important when assessing the financial performance and cash generation of a business.

## 5. Formatting

- Use bold formatting for operating income and EBITDA.
- Apply borders to distinguish key subtotals.
- Keep number formats consistent across forecast years.
- Remove temporary highlighting once all calculations are complete.

## Key Takeaways

- Operating income equals gross profit minus total operating expenses.
- EBITDA adds depreciation and amortisation back to operating income.
- EBITDA is a useful performance measure, but it is not a direct measure of cash flow.
- Accurate calculations depend on linking depreciation and amortisation correctly.
