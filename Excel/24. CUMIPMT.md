# CUMIPMT Function in Excel

## 1. What Is the CUMIPMT Function?

The **CUMIPMT (Cumulative Interest Payment)** function calculates the total interest paid on a loan between two specified payment periods.

Unlike the IPMT function, which calculates the interest for a single payment period, CUMIPMT calculates the accumulated interest across a range of periods.

For example, you can use CUMIPMT to calculate:
- The total interest paid during the first month of a loan.
- The total interest paid during the second year of a loan.
- The total interest paid over any specified range of payment periods.

It is useful when analyzing the interest cost of a loan over time.

## 2. CUMIPMT Function Syntax

```excel
=CUMIPMT(rate, nper, pv, start_period, end_period, type)
```

### Arguments

| Argument | Description | Required? |
|---|---|---|
| `rate` | The interest rate for each payment period. | Yes |
| `nper` | The total number of payment periods over the loan's duration. | Yes |
| `pv` | The present value, or the original amount borrowed. | Yes |
| `start_period` | The first payment period to include in the interest calculation. | Yes |
| `end_period` | The last payment period to include in the interest calculation. | Yes |
| `type` | Specifies when payments are due: `0` means at the end of each period; `1` means at the beginning. | Yes |

**Important:** All six arguments are required. Unlike some other financial functions, CUMIPMT does not allow you to omit the `type` argument.

## 3. Understanding the Loan Information

In the practice file, the following cells contain the loan information:

| Cell | Description |
|---|---|
| `B2` | Annual interest rate |
| `B3` | Loan term in years |
| `B4` | Original loan amount |
| `B5` | Result for cumulative interest over the second year |
| `B6` | Result for cumulative interest during the first month |

The examples in this lesson use monthly payments.

### Convert the Annual Interest Rate

Because payments are monthly, divide the annual interest rate by 12:

```excel
=B2/12
```

This converts the annual interest rate into a monthly rate.

### Calculate the Total Number of Payments

Multiply the loan term in years by 12:

```excel
=B3*12
```

For example, a three-year loan with monthly payments has:

`3 × 12 = 36 payments`

The interest rate and total number of payments must match the payment frequency.

## 4. Example 1: Calculating Interest Paid During the Second Year

Suppose you want to calculate the total interest paid during the second year of a loan with monthly payments.

The second year includes months **13 through 24**.

### Step 1: Select the Cell

Click cell `B5`, where you want the cumulative interest result to appear.

### Step 2: Enter the Formula

Using the worksheet cells from the lesson, enter:

```excel
=CUMIPMT(B2/12, B3*12, B4, 13, 24, 0)
```

### Step 3: Understand the Arguments

- `B2/12` — the monthly interest rate.
- `B3*12` — the total number of monthly payments.
- `B4` — the original loan amount.
- `13` — the first payment period to include.
- `24` — the last payment period to include.
- `0` — payments occur at the end of each period.

### Step 4: Press Enter

Excel calculates the cumulative interest paid from month 13 through month 24.

The result will generally be negative when the loan amount is entered as a positive value. This reflects interest paid out by the borrower.

**Note:** This formula calculates the interest for the second year only, not the total interest paid over the entire two-year period.

## 5. Example 2: Calculating Interest Paid During the First Month

You can also use CUMIPMT to calculate the interest paid during a single payment period.

To calculate the interest for the first month, set both the starting and ending periods to `1`.

Enter the following formula in cell `B6`:

```excel
=CUMIPMT(B2/12, B3*12, B4, 1, 1, 0)
```

### Understanding the Arguments

- `B2/12` — the monthly interest rate.
- `B3*12` — the total number of monthly payments.
- `B4` — the original loan amount.
- `1` — the first payment period.
- `1` — the last payment period.
- `0` — payments occur at the end of each period.

Because both period arguments are `1`, Excel calculates the interest for the first month only.

## 6. Understanding the Type Argument

The `type` argument specifies when payments are due.

| Type | Payment Timing |
|---|---|
| `0` | Payments occur at the end of each period. |
| `1` | Payments occur at the beginning of each period. |

For a typical loan with monthly repayments at the end of each month, use `0`.

For a loan with payments due at the beginning of each period, use `1`.

**Important:** The `type` argument is required. Leaving it out may cause Excel to return an error.

## 7. Understanding Negative Results

CUMIPMT commonly returns a negative result when the original loan amount is entered as a positive value.

This happens because Excel treats the original loan amount as money received and interest payments as money paid out.

For example, a result of `-1,200` means that 1,200 units of currency were paid in interest over the selected periods.

The negative sign does not indicate an error.

## 8. Formatting the Result

You may want to display the cumulative interest as a number or a monetary amount.

To change the format:

1. Select the cell containing the CUMIPMT formula.
2. Go to the **Home** tab.
3. Find the **Number** group.
4. Choose an appropriate format, such as **Number** or **Currency**.
5. Adjust the decimal places if necessary.

In Excel for the web, check the number format if the result does not display as expected.

Formatting changes how the result appears, not the underlying calculation.

## 9. CUMIPMT vs. IPMT

Both functions calculate loan interest, but they serve different purposes.

| Function | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `IPMT` | Calculates interest for one specific payment period. | Interest paid in month 1. |
| `CUMIPMT` | Calculates total interest across a specified range of payment periods. | Interest paid from month 13 through month 24. |

For example, to calculate interest for the first month using IPMT:

```excel
=IPMT(B2/12, 1, B3*12, B4)
```

To calculate cumulative interest for that same month using CUMIPMT:

```excel
=CUMIPMT(B2/12, B3*12, B4, 1, 1, 0)
```

Both formulas calculate interest for the first payment period, but CUMIPMT can also calculate interest across multiple periods by changing the start and end period arguments.

## 10. Important Things to Remember

- **CUMIPMT calculates cumulative interest.** It adds up the interest over the selected payment periods.
- **All six arguments are required.** This includes the `type` argument.
- **Match the rate to the payment frequency.** For monthly payments, divide the annual interest rate by 12 when the rate is quoted annually.
- **Calculate the total number of payments correctly.** Multiply the number of years by the number of payments per year.
- **Choose the correct start and end periods.** For the second year of monthly payments, use periods 13 through 24.
- **Negative results are normal.** They represent interest paid by the borrower.
- **Use the correct `type` value.** Use `0` for payments at the end of each period and `1` for payments at the beginning.
- **CUMIPMT calculates interest only.** It does not calculate the principal portion or the total loan payment.

## 11. Excel Version Compatibility

- Excel for the web.

## 12. Summary

The CUMIPMT function calculates the total interest paid on a loan between two payment periods.

Its syntax is:

```excel
=CUMIPMT(rate, nper, pv, start_period, end_period, type)
```

For the second year of a loan with monthly payments, the example formula is:

```excel
=CUMIPMT(B2/12, B3*12, B4, 13, 24, 0)
```

For the first month only, use:

```excel
=CUMIPMT(B2/12, B3*12, B4, 1, 1, 0)
```

**In summary:** Use CUMIPMT when you need to calculate the total interest paid across a range of loan payment periods. It is especially useful for analyzing interest costs over a particular month, year, or other part of a loan's repayment schedule.
